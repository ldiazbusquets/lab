The market

Simulated, with moods

Prices come from a generator with hidden moods: calm, bull and bear. Each mood lasts weeks or months, and bear markets are more volatile with occasional crashes. That gives trends and volatility clusters a real but imperfect edge, as in real markets, without pretending to forecast any real stock.

Q-learning

Learning by trial and error

The agent sees only the trend, recent momentum, volatility and its own position. For every situation it keeps a score for each allocation, the Q-value. After each day it compares what happened with what it expected and nudges the score toward the result. Early on it explores at random; it gradually trusts what it has learned.

The honest caveat

Why test years matter

Results are always measured on 40 years the agent never trained on. An agent that only looks good on its training data has memorised noise. Real markets are far harder: patterns are weaker, change over time and get competed away once found.
