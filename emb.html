Embeddings

An embedding turns a word, a sentence or an image into a list of numbers, chosen so that things with similar meanings get similar numbers. It is how AI measures "closeness of meaning", and it powers search, recommendations and chatting with your own documents. This page uses real word embeddings for 8,000 English words.

Every word gets its own list of numbers. Similar words get similar lists.

Similarity

Each stripe is one word's embedding: 50 numbers, shown as blue (positive) and amber (negative) cells. No single number means anything you could name. The meaning is in the overall pattern.

Compare two words and the similarity score measures how closely their patterns line up, from 100% (pointing the same way) down to zero or below (unrelated). Try the presets, or type any common English word.

A surprise

happy and sad score as quite similar. The embeddings were learned from which words appear near each other in text, and "happy" and "sad" show up in the same kinds of sentences. Similar here means "used in similar ways", which is close to meaning, but not the same.

02The map of meaningNearest neighbours

Lay the words out by their numbers and meaning turns into geography.

80 words, placed by their embeddingsHover a word

No one told the model which words are countries, animals or colours. Yet when the words are placed according to their embeddings, they gather into neighbourhoods on their own. Cities sit near their countries, sports near each other, numbers in a tight cluster.

The real embeddings have 50 dimensions; this map squashes them into 2, so distances on it are only approximate. A searched word is drawn at the middle of its closest neighbours on the map.

03Word arithmeticDirections mean things

Because meaning is stored as numbers, you can add and subtract it.

− +

Take the numbers for king, subtract man, add woman, and find the word whose numbers are closest to the result. The answer is queen. The step from "man" to "woman" points in roughly the same direction everywhere in the space, and so does the step from a country to its capital, or from a country to its currency.

Try the presets or type your own. It does not always work; it is a side effect of learning from word patterns, not a rule anyone programmed.

The uncomfortable one

Try doctor − man + woman. The top answer is "nurse". The model learned that from millions of real sentences, so it absorbed the stereotypes in them too. Bias in AI often starts exactly here, in the data, which is why module 14 covers how it is measured and reduced.

04Search by meaningThe first half of RAG

Keyword search needs the same words. Embedding search needs the same meaning.

Keyword searchshared words
    Embedding searchclosest meaning

      The 12 help articles are written as examples for this page. Each one, and your question, is turned into a single embedding by averaging the embeddings of its words.

      A client asking about moving a pension after quitting their job shares no words with the article titled "rolling over a retirement plan from a former employer", so keyword search finds nothing. Embedding search turns both into numbers and finds that they point the same way.

      This is the retrieval step behind tools that "chat with your documents", called RAG and covered in module 12. Averaging word embeddings, as this page does, is crude and makes mistakes. Real systems use models built to embed whole sentences and paragraphs, but the idea is identical.

      Where you meet them

      Search engines, Spotify and Netflix recommendations, fraud detection (transactions that sit far from a customer's usual neighbourhood) and every "chat with your files" tool. The specialised databases that store and search them are called vector databases.
